A month before graduation, I picked up a custom engagement ring. We graduated on June 20, and I was going to propose in July, a month after. We had been together for three years, and I had already built the next part of my life around her.

On July 8, before I got the chance, we broke up. It was because of a mistake I made. I won't pretend otherwise, and I won't write around it. The ring is still in its box.

This post isn't about her. It's about what I did to myself in the months after, and what finally made me stop.

## The weekend I wasn't there

The day after we fought and broke up, I left for a trip I had already planned with my family. I told myself it was the right call, that I needed space, that the plans were already set.

That Sunday night, she came to my place. I wasn't there.

I think about that more than anything else from those weeks. Maybe we would have talked. Maybe nothing would have changed. I'll never know, and not knowing turned into a guilt that followed me everywhere. I had made the mistake that ended things, and then I wasn't home on the one night she showed up.

## Losing myself

For the next few weeks, I fell apart. I went back to my old vices, drinking and smoking, and they took over quickly. Every day looked the same: drink, smoke, call her, call her again. That was the routine.

I stopped talking to my family. I stopped talking to my friends. I had given everything to the relationship for three years, and somewhere along the way I had forgotten that I had a life of my own. When the relationship ended, it felt like there was nothing left underneath it. There were days I lost hope entirely and honestly believed it was the end of my world.

## Going back to work, badly

After a few weeks, I went back to work. I wasn't ready, and it showed.

I couldn't focus. My performance went downhill fast. Tasks I used to finish in an afternoon took me more than a day, even with AI-assisted coding doing a lot of the heavy lifting. I would sit in front of the editor and read the same lines over and over without anything going in.

Instead of hiding it, I asked my client for an ultimatum: give me time to fix my performance and my focus, and hold me to it. Then I started seeing a psychologist once a week. It was expensive, and there were weeks I questioned whether I could keep paying for it. I kept going anyway, and it helped more than I expected. I got better, a lot better. For a while, I thought the worst part was behind me.

## Finding out

This month, I found out I had been lied to about something that mattered to me, long after we had both accepted it was over.

Everything I had rebuilt over those weeks of therapy came down at once. And I went straight back to the bottle.

## The worst morning

Early one morning, drunk, I went to her place. The alcohol made me feel brave and angry, and I wasn't thinking about anything except getting answers. It ended badly, and I left with a deep vertical laceration on my forehead.

I was alone and bleeding, and nobody was coming to help me, because I had made sure nobody knew where I was. I got myself to the hospital and had the wound stitched. Before the sun was fully up, I messaged my client to say I couldn't work that morning, and asked for two weeks off. They said yes without making me explain more than I could. I'm grateful for how supportive they've been through all of this.

## What I understand now

Sitting with stitches on my forehead, I finally saw it clearly.

**Chasing the past isn't worth it.** Every call, every drunk decision, every attempt to get back something that was already gone cost me more than the last. The relationship ended in July. I kept paying for it until September.

**Vices only dig the hole deeper.** Drinking didn't numb anything. It just made every bad situation worse and every bad decision easier. The worst morning of this whole thing only happened because I was drunk enough to think it was a good idea.

**I forgot I had my own life.** I put so much of myself into one person that when she left, I didn't know who I was without her. That's not love I want to repeat. That's losing yourself and calling it devotion.

**Guilt doesn't undo anything.** I made a mistake, and I'll own it. But carrying it every day, and punishing myself with it, didn't fix what happened. It only kept me stuck.

## Starting over, slowly

I'm not writing this from the other side. I'm writing it from somewhere in the middle.

I've decided to stop chasing what's already gone. I'm working on myself, one day at a time. I'm reaching back out to my family and my friends, the people I shut out when I needed them most. I'm getting my focus back at work, and I want to earn back the trust my client showed me when they had every reason not to.

The scar on my forehead will stay. I think I'm okay with that. It's a reminder of where the last few months led me, and of the version of myself I don't want to go back to.

If you're somewhere in the middle of something like this, tell someone. Talk to your family, your friends, or a professional. I waited too long to do that, and I don't recommend it.
